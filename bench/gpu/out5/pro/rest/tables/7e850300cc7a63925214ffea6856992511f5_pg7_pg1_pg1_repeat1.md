The maximum adsorption capacities of MWCNTs and SWCNTs were found to be 2.83 and 2.4 mg/g, respectively. It was obvious that the MWCNT adsorbent was more effective in removing fluoride from aqueous solutions. This could be due to the complex formation with MWCNTs as referenced earlier but a more likely explanation is the higher availability of internally available adsorption sites on MWCNTs due to the different pore size diameters resulting in a larger internal available surface area than for SWCNTs [43]. The adsorption of fluoride onto carbon is usually by physical adsorption and is mainly dependent on two parameters, the specific surface area and the pore size distribution. In the case of the two CNTs, the SWCNTs have a significantly larger surface area of 700 m 2 /g but

have a slightly lower maximum fluoride adsorption capacity of 2.4 mg/g. The pore entry diameter limits for the SWCNTs may account for the relatively low fluoride adsorption capacity of this 700 m 2 /g material.
