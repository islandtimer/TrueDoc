2 Homotopy of $\mathcal{C}_p(G)$

In this section, we review some of the standard facts of order complexes of posets. See the first two parts of Smith's book[9] for more details.

Let $(\mathcal{P}, \leq)$ be a poset (short for partially ordered set). The order complex $\Delta\mathcal{P}$ of $\mathcal{P}$ consists of all finite chains in $\mathcal{P}$ , including the empty set. The chain of length $i$ in $\mathcal{P}$ consisting of $i + 1$ elements is called an $i$ -simplex in $\Delta\mathcal{P}$ . In particular, the empty set $\emptyset$ is the $(-1)$ -simplex. It is well-known that such (abstract) simplicial complex $\Delta\mathcal{P}$ can be embedded into a Euclidean topological space as a subspace denoted by $|\Delta\mathcal{P}|$ , so-called the geometric realization of $\Delta\mathcal{P}$ . We abbreviate $|\Delta\mathcal{P}|$ as $|\mathcal{P}|$ here.

A poset map $f : (\mathcal{P}, \leq_{\mathcal{P}}) \rightarrow (\mathcal{Q}, \leq_{\mathcal{Q}})$ of posets $\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{Q}$ , is a set-theoretical map such that $x \leq_{\mathcal{P}} y$ implies $f(x) \leq_{\mathcal{Q}} f(y)$ . It is routine to check that a poset map $f : \mathcal{P} \rightarrow \mathcal{Q}$ naturally induces a simplicial map $\Delta f$ between order complexes $\Delta\mathcal{P} and \Delta\mathcal{Q} , and f$ also induces a continuous map $|f| : |\mathcal{P}| \rightarrow |\mathcal{Q}|$ . The following lemma gives a sufficient condition for the homotopy between two continuous maps induced by poset maps.

Lemma 2. [9, Lemma 3.1.7] Suppose that $f, g : P \rightarrow Q$ satisfy $f, g$ are poset maps with $f \leq g$ , that is, $f(x) \leq g(x)$ for each $x \in P$ . Then $f and g$ are homotopic, that is, $|f|, |g| : |\mathcal{P}| \rightarrow |\mathcal{Q}|$ are homotopic.

Recall that topological spaces $X and Y$ are homotopy equivalent if there are continuous maps $f : X \rightarrow Y and g : Y \rightarrow X$ whose compositions satisfy the homotopies $g \circ f \simeq \text{Id}_X and f \circ g \simeq \text{Id}_Y$ . For posets $\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{Q} , \mathcal{P} and \mathcal{Q}$ are said to be homotopy-equivalent if $|\mathcal{P}| and |\mathcal{Q}|$ are homotopy-equivalent, simply denoted by $\mathcal{P} \simeq \mathcal{Q}$ .

For a prime $p$ and a group $G$ , we denote

$$\mathcal{I}_p(G) = \{P_1 \cap P_2 \cdots \cap P_s \mid P_i \in \text{Syl}_p(G) \text{ for all } 1 \leq i \leq s, \text{ and } s \geq 1\}$$

by the set of all intersections of some Sylow $p$ -subgroups of $G$ .

Lemma 3. Let $G$ be a group and $p$ be a prime. Write

$$\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}_p(G)) = \{Hx \mid H \in \mathcal{I}_p(G), x \in G\}.$$

Then $\mathcal{C}_p(G)$ is homotopy-equivalent to $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}_p(G))$ .

Proof. For each $Hx \in \mathcal{C}_p(G)$ , we define a map $f$ from $\mathcal{C}_p(G)$ to $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}_p(G))$ by

$$f(Hx) := \bigcap_{H \subseteq P \in \text{Syl}_p(G)} Px = \left( \bigcap_{H \subseteq P \in \text{Syl}_p(G)} P \right) x.$$
