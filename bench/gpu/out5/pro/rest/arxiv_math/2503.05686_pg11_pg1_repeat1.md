$$\begin{aligned} & \varepsilon \mu_{1,2} \lambda_{1,2} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \left| \int_0^t F(r, t) \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \int_0^\infty \left( S_3(s) (f_1 f_2 - \tilde{f}_1 \tilde{f}_2) \right) (v_1, v_2, v_3) ds dv_3 dv_2 dr \right| dv_1 \\ & \leq \varepsilon \lambda_{1,2} \int_0^t \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} F(r, t) \left| f_1 f_2 - \tilde{f}_1 \tilde{f}_2 \right| dv_1 dv_2 dv_3 dr \\ & \leq \varepsilon \lambda_{1,2} \int_0^t \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \left( \left| f_1 - \tilde{f}_1 \right| f_2 - \left| f_2 - \tilde{f}_2 \right| \tilde{f}_1 \right) dv_1 dv_2 dv_3 dr \\ & \leq \varepsilon t \lambda_{1,2} \sup_{0 < r < t} \left( M_2(r) \left\| f_1(\cdot, r) - \tilde{f}_1(\cdot, r) \right\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R})} + M_1(r) \left\| f_2(\cdot, \cdot, r) - \tilde{f}_2(\cdot, \cdot, r) \right\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^2)} \right), \end{aligned}$$

and

\begin{aligned} & \mu_{2,1} \lambda_{1,2} \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \left| \int_0^t \left( S_2((t-s)/\varepsilon) \int_{\mathbb{R}} \int_0^\infty \left( S_3(r) (f_1 f_2 - \tilde{f}_1 \tilde{f}_2) \right) (\cdot, \cdot, v_3) dr dv_3 \right) (v_1, v_2) ds \right| dv_2 dv_1 \\ & = \lambda_{1,2} \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \left| \int_0^t \int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{-\mu_{1,1} \frac{(t-s)}{\varepsilon} - \lambda_{1,2} \int_0^t M_1(q) dq} (f_1 f_2 - \tilde{f}_1 \tilde{f}_2) dv_3 ds \right| dv_2 dv_1 \\ & \leq t \lambda_{1,2} \sup_{0 < s < t} \left( M_1(s) \left\| f_2(\cdot, \cdot, s) - \tilde{f}_2(\cdot, \cdot, s) \right\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^2)} + M_2(s) \left\| f_1(\cdot, s) - \tilde{f}_1(\cdot, s) \right\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^2)} \right). \end{aligned}$$

Convergence of $(M_1(t), M_2(t))$ to $(M_1^\infty, M_2^\infty)$ as $t \rightarrow \infty$ (see (21)) allows to iterate the contraction estimate and implies a global $L^1$ -bound of $(f_1, f_2)$ and therefore global existence. $\square$

Remark 3.1. We notice that the third estimated term, part of the right-hand-side of $f_2$ , shows a term with constant proportional to $\frac{t}{\varepsilon}$ . This is a consequence of the fact that the dynamics of $f_2$ are much faster than the ones of $f_1$ and indicates that time-uniform estimates for the fast variable $f_2$ break down for vanishing $\varepsilon$ . Clearly the limits $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0 and t \rightarrow 0$ do not commute. Thus, we expect difficulties arising taking the the limit $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$ for small time $t$ .
