For a set $X \subseteq V(G)$ , the (open) neighborhood of $X$ , denoted by $N_G(X)$ , is the set $\{v \in V(G) \setminus X : v \text{ is adjacent to a vertex in } X\}$ . The closed neighborhood of $X$ is the set $N_G[X] = N_G(X) \cup X$ . We simply use $N(v), N[v], N(X) , and N[X]$ to denote $N_G(v), N_G[v], N_G(X) , and N_G[X]$ , respectively when the context of the graph $G$ is clear. We use $G[X]$ to denote the subgraph of $G$ induced by the vertices in $X$ . A vertex $v$ dominates itself and all its neighbors. A set $X \subseteq V(G)$ is called a clique of $G$ if every pair of vertices of $X$ are adjacent. For disjoint subsets $X and Y$ of $V(G) , G[X, Y]$ denotes the set of edges $\{e \in E(G) : e \text{ has one endpoint in } X \text{ and the other endpoint in } Y\}$ . If the graph $G$ is clear from the context, we write $[X, Y]$ rather than $G[X, Y]$ . We say that $G[X, Y]$ is complete if every vertex in $X$ is adjacent to every vertex in $Y$ in $G$ . For a given positive integer $k$ , we use the notation $[k]$ to denote the set $\{1, \dots, k\}$ .

For a set $D \subseteq V(G)$ and a vertex $u \in D$ , the D-external private neighborhood of $u$ , denoted by $\text{epn}(u, D)$ , is the set $\{v \in V(G) \setminus D : N(v) \cap D = \{u\}\}$ . A vertex in $\text{epn}(u, D)$ is a D-external private neighbor of $u$ . We say that a vertex $v \in V(G) \setminus D$ is D-defended if there exists a vertex $u \in N(v) \cap D$ such that $(D \setminus \{u\}) \cup \{v\}$ is a dominating set of $G$ . Specifically, we say that $v \in V(G) \setminus D$ is D-defended by a vertex $u$ if $u \in N(v) \cap D and (D \setminus \{u\}) \cup \{v\}$ is a dominating set of $G$ . We note that $D$ is a secure dominating set of $G$ if and only if every vertex in $V(G) \setminus D$ is D-defended . We also note that if $D$ is a dominating set of $G and \text{epn}(u, D) = \emptyset$ for some $u \in D$ , then every neighbor of $u$ in $V(G) \setminus D$ is D-defended by $u$ . We state this observation formally as follows.

Observation 2. If $D$ is a dominating set of a graph $G and \text{epn}(u, D) = \emptyset$ for some vertex $u \in D$ , then every neighbor of $u$ that belongs to $V(G) \setminus D$ is D-defended by $u$ .

As a consequence of Observation 2, if $D$ is a dominating set of $G and \text{epn}(u, D) = \emptyset$ for every $u \in D$ , then every vertex in $V(G) \setminus D$ is D-defended , implying that $D$ is a secure dominating set of $G$ . For a given dominating set $D$ of $G$ , we partition $D$ as $A_D \cup B_D$ , where the sets $A_D and B_D$ are defined as follows.

• $A_D = \{u \in D : \text{there exists a neighbor of } u \text{ in } V(G) \setminus D \text{ that is not } D\text{-defended.}\} (\mathcal{P}_1)$ • $B_D = D \setminus A_D$ .

We now prove the following useful lemma. We remark that some of the facts that appear in the following lemma have appeared implicitly in several papers (see [4, 15]).

Lemma 1. Let $D$ be a dominating set of $G and A_D \cup B_D$ be a partition of $D$ as defined in $(\mathcal{P}_1)$ .

(a) If $A_D = \emptyset$ , then $D$ is a secure dominating set of $G$ . (b) If a vertex $v$ in $V(G) \setminus D$ is not D-defended , then $N(v) \cap D \subseteq A_D$ . (c) If $u$ is a vertex in $A_D$ , then $\text{epn}(u, D) \neq \emptyset$ . (d) If a vertex $v$ in $V(G) \setminus D$ is not D-defended , then for every neighbor $u$ of $v$ in $D$ , the vertex $v$ has a non-neighbor in $\text{epn}(u, D)$ , that is, $\text{epn}(u, D) \setminus N(v) \neq \emptyset$ . (e) If $D'$ is a superset of $D$ , then $A_{D'} \subseteq A_D and B_D \subseteq B_{D'}$ , where $A_{D'} \cup B_{D'}$ is a partition of $D'$ as defined in $(\mathcal{P}_1)$ .

Proof. Parts (a) and (b) follow by the definition of the sets $A_D and B_D$ , while part (c) follows by Observation 2.
