Third, as argued in Sec. II A, every legitimate operation that can be performed without work supply must fall within the scope of conditionally uniformity-covariant operations. Here, we assume that the converse is also true, namely that all such operations can be performed freely, so that the work cost defined under such oper-

ations is equal to the actual work cost in its literal sense. Note that even in a less permissive setting without this assumption, the work cost defined under conditionally uniformity-covariant operations still provides a no-go limit on the actual work cost. Fourth, we accommodate the practical need for approximate conversion, where a bounded error is tolerated.

Formally, for a pair of states $\rho_{AB}, \sigma_{A'B'}$ , we define the one-shot work cost of converting $A$ to $A'$ with error $\varepsilon \in [0, 1]$ as

$$W^\varepsilon(\rho_{AB}; \sigma_{A'B'}) := \frac{1}{\beta_b} \inf_{\substack{A_0, A_1, \\ \mathcal{N}_{A_0AB \rightarrow A_1A'B'} \in \text{CUCO}}} \{\log_2 |A_0| - \log_2 |A_1| : \Delta(|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A_1} \otimes \sigma_{A'B'}, \mathcal{N}_{A_0AB \rightarrow A_1A'B'} [|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A_0} \otimes \rho_{AB}]) \leq \varepsilon\}, \quad (40)$$

where CUCO denotes the set of conditionally uniformity-covariant operations. In Eq. (40), $A_0 and A_1$ are the battery systems representing Alice's initial and final work storage, respectively. A positive work cost means that Alice has to release this amount of work from her storage and invest it into the conversion; a negative work cost means that Alice can extract an amount of work, equal to the absolute value of the work cost, from the conversion and keep it in her storage.
