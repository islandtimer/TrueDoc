Remark 3. According to Remark 2, each $I_{ij}$ can be written as an interval $I_{ij} = [L_{ij}, U_{ij}]$ where $L_{ij} and U_{ij}$ are the lower and upper bounds of the interval, respectively. Also, each $S_{ij}$ (if it is non-empty) is obtained as one of the forms of $S_{ij} = \{L_{ij}\} , S_{ij} = \{U_{ij}\} , S_{ij} = \{L_{ij}, U_{ij}\}$ or $S_{ij} = [L_{ij}, U_{ij}]$ .

Definition 4. For each $j \in \mathcal{J}$ , we define $I_j = \bigcap_{i \in \mathcal{I}} I_{ij}$ . Also, let $S'_{ij} = S_{ij} \cap I_j , \forall i \in \mathcal{I} and \forall j \in \mathcal{J}$ .

Corollary 4. Let $i \in \mathcal{I} and j \in \mathcal{J}$ . If $S'_{ij} \neq \emptyset$ , then $S'_{ij}$ is as one of the forms of $S'_{ij} = \{L_j\} , S'_{ij} = \{U_j\} , S'_{ij} = \{L_j, U_j\}$ or $S'_{ij} = [L_j, U_j]$ , where $L_j and U_j$ are the lower and upper bound of $I_j = [L_j, U_j]$ , respectively.

Proof. From Definition 4 and Remark 3, we have

$$I_j = \bigcap_{i \in \mathcal{I}} I_{ij} = \bigcap_{i \in \mathcal{I}} [L_{ij}, U_{ij}] = \left[ \max_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \{L_{ij}\}, \min_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \{U_{ij}\} \right] = [L_j, U_j]$$

Hence, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ , if $L_{ij} \in I_j$ ( $U_{ij} \in I_j$ ), then we must have $L_{ij} = L_j$ ( $U_{ij} = U_j$ ). On the other hand, from Remark 3, a non-empty $S_{ij}$ is as one of the forms of $S_{ij} = \{L_{ij}\} , S_{ij} = \{U_{ij}\} , S_{ij} = \{L_{ij}, U_{ij}\}$ or $S_{ij} = [L_{ij}, U_{ij}]$ . So, if $S_{ij} = \{L_{ij}\} and S'_{ij} = S_{ij} \cap I_j \neq \emptyset$ , then $L_{ij} \in I_j$ ; that is, $L_{ij} = L_j$ and therefore $S'_{ij} = S_{ij} \cap I_j = \{L_j\}$ . For the other cases, the results are attained by the similar argument. $\square$
