$H_{\pi_0}^2(X)^{\mathbb{T}}$ belonging to the zero $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$ -weight space are represented by log-canonical bi-vector fields, we let

$$\mathcal{S}(\pi_0) = \text{the set of all the non-zero } (\mathbb{C}^\times)^n\text{-weights in } H_{\pi_0}^2(X)^{\mathbb{T}}, \quad (1.2)$$

and we focus on Poisson deformations of $\pi_0$ of the form $\pi = \pi_0 + \pi_1 + \pi'$ , where $\pi_1$ is a sum of $\mathbb{T}$ -invariant algebraic bi-vector fields on $X$ with $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$ -weights in $\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ and satisfies $[\pi_0, \pi_1] = 0 , and \pi'$ is a correction term such that $\pi$ is Poisson. We show in §2.2 (see Lemma 2.8 and Lemma 2.9) that the set $\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ has the following properties:

1) $\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ is a finite subset of $(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq -1})^n$ and each $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ has exactly two entries of $-1$ ; 2) For every $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ , the $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ -weight space of $H_{\pi_0}^2(X)^{\mathbb{T}}$ is 1-dimensional with a basis vector represented by a uniquely defined bi-vector field $V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ on $X$ (see (2.19)).

For $p \in [0, n]$ , let $\mathfrak{X}^p(X)$ be the space of all algebraic $p$ -vector fields on $X$ , and let $[\cdot, \cdot]_{\text{Sch}}$ be the Schouten bracket on $\mathfrak{X}(X) = \bigoplus_{p=0}^n \mathfrak{X}^p(X)$ . For $W \subset \mathbb{Z}^n and p \in [0, n]$ , let $\mathfrak{X}^p(X)^W$ be the direct sum of the $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$ -weight spaces in $\mathfrak{X}^p(X)$ with weights in $W$ . By definition $\mathfrak{X}^p(X)^W = 0$ if $W = \emptyset$ .

For a subset $\mathcal{S}$ of $\mathcal{S}(\pi_0) and m \geq 1$ , let $\mathcal{S}_{\geq m}$ denote the set of all elements in $\mathbb{Z}^n$ that can be written as the sum of $m$ or more elements in $\mathcal{S}$ . We say that $\mathcal{S}$ satisfies Condition (W1) if

$$\mathfrak{X}^1(X)^{\mathcal{S}_{\geq 2}} = 0.$$

Our main results in the first part of the paper are summarized as follows.

Theorem A. Let $\pi_0$ be any $\mathbb{T}$ -log-symplectic log-canonical Poisson structure on $X = \mathbb{C}^n$ , and let $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ .

1) (Theorem 2.21) Suppose that $\mathcal{S}$ is $\mathbb{C}$ -linearly independent. Let $c = (c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}})_{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}}$ be a set of formal commuting parameters, and let $\pi_1^{\mathcal{S}}(c) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}} c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \in \mathfrak{X}^2(X)^{\mathcal{S}}[[c]]$ . Then there exists $(\pi')^{\mathcal{S}}(c) \in \mathfrak{X}^2(X)^{\mathcal{S}_{\geq 2}}[[c]]$ such

$$\pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c) = \pi_0 + \pi_1^{\mathcal{S}}(c) + (\pi')^{\mathcal{S}}(c) \in \mathfrak{X}^2(X)[[c]] \quad (1.3)$$

satisfies $[\pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c), \pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c)]_{\text{Sch}} = 0$ . Moreover, such a $\pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c)$ is unique up to gauge equivalence (Definition 2.19);

2) (Theorem 2.26) If $\mathcal{S}$ satisfies Condition (W1) (it is then $\mathbb{C}$ -linearly independent by Lemma 2.23), for any $c = (c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \in \mathbb{C}^\times)_{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}}$ , there is a unique $\mathbb{T}$ -invariant algebraic Poisson structure $\pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c)$ on $X$ of the form

$$\pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c) = \pi_0 + \pi_1^{\mathcal{S}}(c) + (\pi')^{\mathcal{S}}(c) \in \mathfrak{X}^2(X), \quad (1.4)$$

where $\pi_1^{\mathcal{S}}(c) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}} c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \in \mathfrak{X}^2(X)^{\mathcal{S}} and (\pi')^{\mathcal{S}}(c) \in \mathfrak{X}^2(X)^{\mathcal{S}_{\geq 2}}$ . Moreover, for any other $c' = (c'_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \in \mathbb{C}^\times)_{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}}$ , the two Poisson structures $\pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c) and \pi^{\mathcal{S}}(c')$ coincide after a re-scaling of the coordinates $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ .

When $\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ is itself $\mathbb{C}$ -linearly independent as a subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$ , the $\mathbb{T}$ -invariant formal power series Poisson deformation $\pi^{\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)}(c)$ of $\pi_0$ in (1.3) is said to be maximal , and we explain in Theorem 2.22 the precise sense in which it is maximal. Similarly, if $\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)$ satisfies Condition (W1), the $\mathbb{T}$ -invariant algebraic Poisson deformation $\pi^{\mathcal{S}(\pi_0)}(c)$ of $\pi_0$ in (1.4), for any $c = (c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \in \mathbb{C}^\times)_{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathcal{S}(\pi_0)}$ , is said to be maximal , and the precise sense in which it is maximal is explained in Theorem 2.34.

We remark that the miracle that the Poisson deformations along $\pi_1^{\mathcal{S}}(c)$ as in Theorem A are unobstructed has been observed in [MPS20] in the context of complex log-symplectic manifolds. While the methods of [MPS20] heavily used the homotopy algebra techniques, such $L_\infty$ -algebras, our proofs are elementary and our strategies are adopted from [MPS24] where deformations of $q$ -symmetric algebras are studied.
