4.2 Definition and Properties of $T_{d,n}$

In this section we recall some properties of the $T_{d,n}$ spaces introduced by Chen, Gibney, and Krashen [CGK09]. We also introduce a functor of points for these spaces and use this to define an operad with spaces $\{T_{d,n}\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ .

If $\mathrm{pr}_i: X^n \rightarrow X$ denotes the projection to the $i$ :th component it is clear from definitions that the composition

$$D([n]) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{FM}_n^{\mathrm{alg}}(X) \xrightarrow{\rho} X^n \xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}_i} X$$

is independent of the integer $i$ . We denote this morphism by $\pi: D([n]) \rightarrow X$ . In [CGK09], Chen, Gibney, and Krashen show that for a fixed $n$ , any smooth scheme $X$ of dimension $d$ , and any point $x \in X$ , the fibers $\pi^{-1}(x)$ are isomorphic. We define $T_{d,n}$ as $\pi^{-1}(0)$ for the map $\pi: D([n]) \rightarrow \mathbb{A}^d$ but by the above we could equally well have taken any other smooth variety instead of $X = \mathbb{A}^d$ .

Remark. Just like the topological case notice that while we have up until this point indexed coordinates with $[n]$ we are free to use any finite index set $I and \mathrm{FM}_I^{\mathrm{alg}}(X) and T_{d,I}$ together with all related maps and divisors are defined in the analogous way.

The variety $T_{d,n}$ is smooth and comes equipped with a smooth closed subscheme $T_{d,n}(I)$ for every non empty $I \subseteq [n]$ . For $I \in P_2(n)$ , i.e. $I \neq [n] and |I| \geq 2$ , we define $T_{d,n}(I)$ as the pullback of $D(I) \subseteq \mathrm{FM}_n^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ to $T_{d,n}$ . For other $I$ (i.e. $I$ is $[n]$ or a one point set) we take $T_{d,n}(I) := T_{d,n}$ . Similarly, for a collection of (distinct) subsets $I_1, \dots, I_k \subseteq [n]$ we define $T_{d,n}(I_1, \dots, I_k) := \bigcap_i T_{d,n}(I_i)$ . For $I \neq [n] and |I| \geq 2$ the subscheme $T_{d,n}(I)$ is a smooth divisor of $T_{d,n}$ and the union of all these divisors is a smooth normal crossings divisor. Additionally, the intersection $T_{d,n}(I_1, \dots, I_k)$ is non empty if and only if $(I_1, \dots, I_k)$ is nested . All this essentially follows from the analogous results for $\mathrm{FM}_n^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ recalled in Section 4.1. Details can be found in [CGK09].

Remark. The reason we include definitions of $T_{d,n}(\{i\}) and T_{d,n}([n])$ is that this will simplify the notation for some later results.
