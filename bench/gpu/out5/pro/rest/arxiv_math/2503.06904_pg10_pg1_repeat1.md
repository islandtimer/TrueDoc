$$(2.8) \quad U(z) = \frac{1}{|z|} U \left( \frac{z}{|z|^2} \right), \quad U_j(z) = \frac{1}{|z|} U_j \left( \frac{z}{|z|^2} \right), \quad j = 1, \dots, m,

and

(2.9) \quad \zeta_j(z) = \zeta_j \left( \frac{z}{|z|^2} \right), \quad j = 1, \dots, m.$$

Together with the properties that the ansatz is $2\pi/m$ -rotationally invariant in $z_1 z_2$ plane and even symmetry with respect to the $z_3$ axis, one can solve $\psi$ from (2.7) uniquely in a suitable Sobolev space, which is the orthogonal complement of the space spanned by the kernels of the linear operator $\Delta + 5U^4$ . More precisely, the authors in [20] proved that

$$\|\psi\|_{L_w^\infty} \leq C \left( \|V(z)\psi\|_{L_w^q} + 5 \left\| |U_*|^4 \sum_j (1 - \zeta_j) \tilde{\phi}_j \right\|_{L_w^q} + \|M(\psi)\|_{L_w^q} \right),$$

where

$$\|\psi\|_{L_w^\infty} := \|(1 + |z|)\psi\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^3)} \quad \text{and} \quad \|h\|_{L_w^q} := \left\| (1 + |z|)^{5-\frac{6}{q}} h \right\|_{L^q(\mathbb{R}^3)}.$$

Particularly, for $q > \frac{3}{2}$ it has been shown that

$$\left\| \left( 1 - \sum_{j=1}^m \zeta_j \right) N \left( \sum_{j=1}^m \tilde{\phi}_j + \psi \right) \right\|_{L_w^q} + \left\| |U_*|^4 \sum_j (1 - \zeta_j) \tilde{\phi}_j \right\|_{L_w^q} + \|V(z)\psi\|_{L_w^q} \leq \frac{C}{(\log m)^2}.$$
