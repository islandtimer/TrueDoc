Still, the formulation of global hyperbolicity would be rather indirect. We would prefer a formulation phrased directly in terms of continuity properties of $d$ .

To start with, we shall explore some causality properties expressed via the Lorentzian distance function.

Proposition 2.1. On a spacetime $(M, g)$ for any two events $p, q \in M$ , the inclusion $I^+(q) \subset I^+(p)$ is equivalent to the property $d_q \leq d_p$ . Dually, the inclusion $I^-(p) \subset I^-(q)$ is equivalent to the property $d^p \leq d^q$ .

This result is a bit surprising since the function $d$ contains much more information than the chronological relation. Note that we do not assume that $d$ is finite or continuous.

Proof. Suppose that $I^+(q) \subset I^+(p)$ and let $r \in M$ . If $r \notin I^+(q)$ then $d_q(r) = 0$ and the inequality we wish to prove follows. If $r \in I^+(q)$ for any $m > 0 , m < d_q(r)$ , there is a timelike curve $\gamma : [0, 1] \rightarrow M$ connecting $q = \gamma(0)$ to $r = \gamma(1)$ of Lorentzian length $\ell(\gamma) > m$ . Let $\delta > 0$ be such that $\ell(\gamma|_{[\delta, 1]}) > m$ . But $\gamma(\delta) \gg q$ hence $\gamma(\delta) \gg p$ , which implies $d_p(r) \geq \ell(\gamma|_{[\delta, 1]}) > m$ . Since

$$m < d_q(r)$$

is arbitrary, $d_p(r) \geq d_q(r)$ . By the arbitrariness of $r , d_q \leq d_p$ .

For the converse, suppose that $d_q \leq d_p$ , then if $r \in I^+(q)$ we have $d_q(r) > 0$ so $d_p(r) > 0$ which implies $r \in I^+(p)$ . By the arbitrariness of $r , I^+(q) \subset I^+(p)$ . $\square$

We recall [43, Def. 4.46] that a spacetime is future distinguishing if $I^+(p) = I^+(q) \Rightarrow p = q$ (and similarly in the past case). A spacetime is weakly distinguishing if $I^+(p) = I^+(q) and I^-(p) = I^-(q) \Rightarrow p = q$ , cf. [43, Def. 4.47]. A spacetime is distinguishing if $I^+(p) = I^+(q)$ or $I^-(p) = I^-(q) \Rightarrow p = q$ , cf. [43, Def. 4.59].

It is convenient to introduce the following notions

Definition 2.2. We say that the Lorentzian distance future distinguishes events (the spacetime is future $d$ -distinguishing) if $d_p = d_q \Rightarrow p = q$ , and that is past distinguishes events if $d^p = d^q \Rightarrow p = q$ . The spacetime is $d$ -distinguishing if it is both future and past $d$ -distinguishing, namely: $d^p = d^q$ or $d_p = d_q \Rightarrow p = q$ . We say that the spacetime is weakly $d$ -distinguishing if $d^p = d^q and d_p = d_q \Rightarrow p = q$ .

Note that $d$ -distinction implies ‘future or past $d$ -distinction’ which implies weak $d$ -distinction.

Remark 2.3. Weak $d$ -distinction is called just $d$ -distinction (or it is said that the Lorentzian distance distinguishes events) in [45, 13] as this is the only version that plays a role in those works.

An immediate consequence of Prop. 2.1 is

Corollary 2.4. On a spacetime $(M, g)$ (future/past/weak) $d$ -distinction is equivalent to (resp. future/past/weak) distinction.
