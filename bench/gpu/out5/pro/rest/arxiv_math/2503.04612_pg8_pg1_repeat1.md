is continuous. Since the projective space $\mathbb{RP}^1$ is compact, the function is bounded.

Given $\omega = (\omega)_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \in \Omega$ , write $\omega = (\omega_-, \omega_+)$ , where $\omega_- = (\omega)_{n < 0} and \omega_+ = (\omega)_{n \geq 0}$ . For one-step cocycles, the first Oseledets direction $\mathbf{E}_1(\omega)$ depends only on $\omega_-$ , while the second direction $\mathbf{E}_2(\omega)$ depends only on $\omega_+$ . So we can denote them as $\mathbf{E}_1(\omega_-) and \mathbf{E}_2(\omega_+)$ . Let $\mu_{\pm}$ denote the push-forward of $\mu$ under the projection $\omega \mapsto \omega_{\pm}$ . Then $\mu$ coincides with the product measure $\mu_- \otimes \mu_+$ . Therefore, by Fubini–Tonelli theorem, $\int |\log \sin \angle(\mathbf{E}_1, \mathbf{E}_2)| d\mu$ equals the double integral

$$(32) \quad \int \int |\log \sin \angle(\mathbf{E}_1(\omega_-), \mathbf{E}_2(\omega_+))| d\mu_-(\omega_-) d\mu_+(\omega_+)$$

which is finite since the inner integral is bounded. This concludes the proof of Theorem 2 in the strongly irreducible case.

Next, we must deal with the cases where $\Gamma_\nu$ permutes a finite set $S$ of one-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^2$ .

If $\#S \geq 3$ , then a cross-ratio argument shows that the projection of $\Gamma_\nu$ to $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ is finite. This implies that $\Gamma_\nu$ consists of linear conformal mappings with respect to some inner product on $\mathbb{R}^2$ , which is incompatible with the hypothesis $\lambda_1(\nu) > \lambda_2(\nu)$ .

If $\#S = 2$ , then the hypothesis $\lambda_1(\nu) > \lambda_2(\nu)$ implies that $\{\mathbf{E}_1(\omega), \mathbf{E}_2(\omega)\} = S \mu$ -a.e., and in particular the angle between Oseledets subspaces is log-integrable.

It remains to consider the case $\#S = 1$ . Taking inverses we may assume that the stable subspace $\mathbf{E}_2(\omega)$ belongs to $S$ for $\mu$ -a.e. $\omega \in \Omega$ . With a change of basis we further assume that all $\nu$ -almost every matrix is upper triangular. Multiplying the cocycle matrices by a log-integrable factor, we can assume that the lower right entry is always 1. That is, the function $F$ is of the form:

$$(33) \quad F(\omega) = \begin{pmatrix} a(\omega_0) & b(\omega_0) \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$

where $\log |a| and \log |b|$ are square-integrable and $\int \log |a| d\pi < 0$ . By Lemma 5, the cocycle has Lyapunov exponents $\lambda_1 = 0 and \lambda_2 = \int \log |a| d\pi$ , and the corresponding Oseledets directions are spanned by the vectors $(X(\omega), 1)^\top and (1, 0)^\top$ , where

$$(34) \quad X(\omega) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a(\omega_{-1})a(\omega_{-2}) \cdots a(\omega_{-n})b(\omega_{-n-1}).$$

Writing $\varphi(\omega_0) := -\log a(\omega_0) , \psi(\omega_0) := \log b(\omega_0)$ , we have:

$$(35) \quad X(\omega) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \pm e^{S_n(\omega)} \quad \text{where} \quad S_n(\omega) := \psi(\omega_{-n-1}) - \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \varphi(\omega_{-i}).$$
