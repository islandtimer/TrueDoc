exist some $t_1$ with $0 < t_1 < t < t_0$ such that $d_X(o, \gamma(t_1)) \leq R$ , contradicting the definition of $t_0$ . Thus, $\gamma(t_0)$ is an accumulation point of $\text{Cone}_o(U) and \gamma(t_0) \in S_X(o, R)$ .

Suppose $p$ is an accumulation point of $\text{Cone}_o(U) and p \in S_X(o, R)$ . Then there exists a sequence $p_n \in \text{Cone}_o(U)$ such that $p_n \rightarrow p$ as $n \rightarrow \infty$ . Let $x_n \in [o, p_n] \cap U$ for some geodesic segment joining $o$ to $p_n$ .

$$\begin{aligned} d_X(x_n, p) &\leq d_X(x_n, p_n) + d_X(p_n, p) \\ &= d_X(o, p_n) - d_X(o, x_n) + d_X(p_n, p) \\ &= d_X(o, p_n) - R + d_X(p_n, p) \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} d_X(o, p) - R + 0 = 0. \end{aligned}$$

Thus $x_n \rightarrow p$ as $n \rightarrow \infty$ . Since $U$ is a connected component of $S$ , it is closed, and we have $p \in U$ . From this above discussion, we get $\gamma(t_0) \in U$ .

Define $t_1 := \sup\{t \geq 0 \mid d_X(o, \gamma(t)) \leq R\}$ , then similarly we can argue that $\gamma(t_1) \in V$ .

Note that

$$\begin{aligned} d_X(\gamma_1(t), \gamma_2(t)) &= d_X(\gamma_1(t), \gamma(t_0)) + d_X(\gamma(t_0), \gamma(t_1)) + d_X(\gamma(t_1), \gamma_2(t)), \\ d_X(o, \gamma_1(t)) &= d_X(o, \gamma_1(R)) + d_X(\gamma_1(R), \gamma_1(t)) = R + d_X(\gamma_1(R), \gamma_1(t)), \\ d_X(o, \gamma_2(t)) &= d_X(o, \gamma_2(R)) + d_X(\gamma_2(R), \gamma_2(t)) = R + d_X(\gamma_2(R), \gamma_2(t)), \end{aligned}$$

which gives,

$$\begin{aligned} 2(\gamma_1(t)|\gamma_2(t))_o &= 2R - d_X(\gamma(t_0), \gamma(t_1)) \\ &\quad + d_X(\gamma_1(t), \gamma(t_0)) - d_X(\gamma_1(R), \gamma_1(t)) \\ &\quad + d_X(\gamma(t_1), \gamma_2(t)) - d_X(\gamma_2(R), \gamma_2(t)). \end{aligned} \tag{42}$$

For $x \in U and y \in V$ we have $2(x|y)_o = d_X(x, o) + d_X(y, o) - d_X(x, y) = 2R - d_X(x, y)$ . Thus from (42) and application of the triangle inequality we get,

$$\begin{aligned} 2|(\gamma_1(t)|\gamma_2(t))_o - (x|y)_o| &\leq |d_X(x, y) - d_X(\gamma(t_0), \gamma(t_1))| \\ &\quad + |d_X(\gamma_1(t), \gamma(t_0)) - d_X(\gamma_1(R), \gamma_1(t))| \\ &\quad + |d_X(\gamma(t_1), \gamma_2(t)) - d_X(\gamma_2(R), \gamma_2(t))| \\ &\leq d_X(x, \gamma(t_0)) + d_X(\gamma(t_1), y) + d_X(\gamma(t_0), \gamma_1(R)) + d_X(\gamma(t_1), \gamma_2(R)) \\ &\leq 2(\text{diam}(U) + \text{diam}(V)) \end{aligned} \tag{43}$$

We know from property of Gromov product space

$$\lim_{t \rightarrow \infty} (\gamma_1(t)|\gamma_2(t))_o = (\xi|\eta)_o.$$

Therefore, from (43) we can conclude the desired claim (40) (by taking limit $t \rightarrow \infty$ on the left hand side).

For proving (41), consider geodesic rays $\gamma_1 and \gamma_2$ joining $o$ to $\xi and \eta$ , respectively, as above. Then $(\gamma_1(t)|\gamma_2(t))_o \uparrow (\xi|\eta)_o$ as $t \rightarrow \infty$ . Note $\gamma_1(R), \gamma_2(R) \in U$ . Thus,

$$(\xi|\eta)_o \geq (\gamma_1(R)|\gamma_2(R))_o = \frac{1}{2} \left( d_X(o, \gamma_1(R)) + d_X(o, \gamma_2(R)) - d_X(\gamma_1(R), \gamma_2(R)) \right) \geq R - \frac{\text{diam}(U)}{2}.$$

□
