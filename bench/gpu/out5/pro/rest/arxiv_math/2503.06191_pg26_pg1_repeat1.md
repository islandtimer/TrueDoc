Corollary 31. Fix $m, n \in \mathbb{N}$ . Let $\psi : \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^+$ be an even $C^1$ function with $\psi, |\nabla \psi|^2 \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n, \gamma_n^{\frac{m}{m+1}}) and D^m \psi, |\nabla D^m \psi|^2 \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^{nm}, \gamma_{n,m})$ . Then,

$$\begin{aligned} & \left( \frac{m+1}{m} \right) \text{Var}_{\gamma_n^{\frac{m}{m+1}}} \psi + \frac{1}{m+1} \text{Var}_{\gamma_{n,m}} D^m \psi \\ & \leq \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{m+1} \int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}} |\nabla D^m \psi(x)|^2 d\gamma_{n,m}(x) + \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |\nabla \psi(x)|^2 d\gamma_n^{\frac{m}{m+1}}(x) \right). \end{aligned}$$

Observe that $\nabla \psi$ refers to taking the gradient of $\psi$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ while $\nabla D^m \psi$ contains the gradient on $\mathbb{R}^{nm}$ . The quantity $|\nabla D^m \psi|^2$ seems a bit mysterious, but in fact it is not so complicated. Since $\nabla$ respects the product structure of $\mathbb{R}^{nm}$ , one has that

$$\nabla D^m \psi(x_1, \dots, x_m) = \left\{ \nabla \psi(x_i) - \nabla \psi \left( - \sum_{i=1}^m x_i \right) \right\}_{i=1}^m.$$

Thus,

$$|\nabla D^m \psi(x_1, \dots, x_m)|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^m \left| \nabla \psi(x_i) - \nabla \psi \left( - \sum_{i=1}^m x_i \right) \right|^2.$$
