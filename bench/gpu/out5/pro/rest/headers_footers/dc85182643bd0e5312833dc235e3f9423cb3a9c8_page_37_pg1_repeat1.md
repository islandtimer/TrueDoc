41, 52], $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ [41, 53-55], $\text{Sr}^{2+}$ [25, 56], $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ [57], etc., the replacement of $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ by $\text{HPO}_4^{2-}$ [58], $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ [40, 41, 59-62] or $\text{SiO}_4^{4-}$ [63-65], and $\text{OH}^-$ by $\text{F}^-$ [29, 41, 66-68] or $\text{Cl}^-$ [69]. In this review, the substitution of different kinds of cations and anions into HA will be extensively studied, with emphasis on the substitution effect on the phase evolution, mechanical and biological properties.

2.3.1 Incorporation of cation into HA

The chemical composition of hexagonal HA (within the ideal $\text{P}6_3/\text{m}$ space group) is $\text{Ca(I)}_4\text{Ca(II)}_6(\text{PO}_4)_6(\text{OH})_2$ (c.f. Figure 2-1). The local atomic configurations are displayed in Figure 2-4. The $\text{Ca(I)}$ site is surrounded by six $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ tetrahedral and coordinated by nine oxygen ions. The $\text{Ca(II)}$ site is seven coordinated with six oxygen ions from $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ and one oxygen ion from $\text{OH}^-$ [70, 71]. It has been reported that the larger cations usually occupy the $\text{Ca(II)}$ sites and the smaller ones prefer the $\text{Ca(I)}$ sites, because the nearest distance among $\text{Ca(I)}$ sites is smaller than that among $\text{Ca(II)}$ sites [72]. The substitution of cations into HA tends to cause the HA structure disordering, and influence the phase, mechanical and biological properties.
