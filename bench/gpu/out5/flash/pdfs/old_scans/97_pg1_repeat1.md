Bailey in charge who instituted himself as watch over the goods until he sent them all up to Head Quarters - My Ambulance came through that PM, but for fear it might not, Genl Sturgis had his taken down for me, and had Suffer arranged and a Splended serenade: I dont know how we could have had a warmer "welcome home" as the officers termed it.

Head Quarters are in the door yard of a farm house, one room of which is occupied by Miss G, and myself. - My wagons are a little away from me out of sight, and I am wishing for a tent and stone to pitch and live near them. - the weather is cold, and the ground covered with snow, but I could make me comfortable with a good tent, floor and stone, and should prefer it to a room in a rebel house and one so generally occupied.

The 21st are a few rods from me, - many of

the officers call to see me every day. Col Clark is very neighborly, he is looking finely now he was in this PM, and was going in search of Col Morse whom he thought to be a mile or two distant; I learned tonight that the 15th are only some three miles away, the 36th I cannot find yet. I have recited hard for them and shall get on track soon I trust.

Of Army movements nothing can be said with certainty, no two persons, not even the Gents agree in reference to the future programme the snow appears to have deranged the plans very seriously. I have recd calls from two Gents, today and in the course of conversation I discovered that their views were entirely different. Genl. Burnide stood a long time in front of my door today but to my astonishment, he did not express his opinion. Strange!
