It is my ardent desire to visit the United States as soon as possible, and give my service to the Government for the suppression of the rebellion, and express to you personally the feeling which your kindness and interest in my welfare have inspired in my heart.

On the receipt of the telegraphic news today that our difficulties with England were pacifically arranged I paid a visit to St. Mc Ship "Pelican" in our harbor and had the happiness to see our glorious flag saluted by the nation, which a few weeks before was ready to deprecate itself our deadly enemy. Oh! how my heart will rejoice when we shall again be a united people, and attract the attention of the nations of the old World by our expanding greatness

and glory, instead of painful criticism upon our national troubles at home and abroad. I wrote to you last month at length, and have only time now to add the warmest expressions of esteem and affection from my father and mother.

I am ever with devoted feelings of friendship

Yours Most Sincerely

J. Judson Barclay
