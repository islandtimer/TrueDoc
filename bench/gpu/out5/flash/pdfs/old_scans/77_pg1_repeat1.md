Individual interests are absorbed in this great National calamity. I know the fact but cannot realize it. I look out upon the same beautiful landscape - the same clear blue sky, the same floating clouds - the face of nature is unchanged - nothing there that indicates that the darkest page in our country's history is now being written in lines of blood? But I turn and one glance on the face of man reveals the terrible certitude of some dark impending war. The 19th of April! Is it not an omen of evil import to those who have dared to raise the hand of rebellion against the common country? For that day 1861 is again made memorable by opening the "Dance of Death" by the shedding the blood of the sons of Mass. as in the days of the Revolution 1775. Dear Madam

Your letter mailed to me on the 11th can duly to hand at a moment when I was more than busy, and as I had just written Mrs. Dickinson (of whom I read the articles) a detailed account of their history and final destination. I have centered with much regret to allow you letter to remain unenvied for a day that I might fair time to write you at greater tenpt. You must before this have learned from any letter to Mrs. D. the occasion of the delay (big uncertain orders, rainy weather and Maryland wolds) and decide with me that the (anxious) package has long before this perfect fulfilled its mission of charity & love.
