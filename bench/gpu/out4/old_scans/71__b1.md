HEREDITY, HOW OPPOSED.

The doctrine of heredity, so ably argued in late numbers of THE ATTRA, deserves further notice, the hints of which have been already given. The Woman-Shell has already touched upon the subject of the birth of children, parents and perverts dedicated to God in behalf of humanity, and has alluded till the last moment along to effect his conversion.

The utmost that parents are encouraged to hope is the early conversion of their children. If the question from or before which they should be admitted a bare householder, who is not in rags, is not accepted as the normal and expected thing, and not expected is not realized. Under a ripened Christianity we shall not hear of the conversion, early or late, of the child, but Christian children, brought up with the germs of divine life implanted in them, to be quickened into activity. Indeed I believe that, to a hopeful extent, such a desire, the case, that of many Christians the practice is rather to hinder than promote, as a general parentage assumes a sanctified nature, and that the reported conversion of children is rather the revivification of a principle afore imparted.

W. W.

"PUSSANCE OBLIGE."

Under the above title, the Cambridge Review of the 2d ult., in an article which well deserves attention, upon the duties of the streets towards the weak, says:

"There exists, our author states, a class of persons to whom its teachings have as yet been very imperfectly applied—why, it would be hard to say, for whatever claims weakness has upon strength, they possess in a marked degree the qualities of the weak. Mr. Lecky, in his History of Ethics, has dubbed them 'the priestesses of immorality,' bearing, since some must bear it, the sin of the world. And who are these impious priestesses?

"They are the women whose evil propensities are born of Christian parents; and perhaps dedicated to God in behalf of humanity, and have till the last moment along to effect his conversion.

"Even when we have had too much respect for womanhood and ourselves, to keep fresh integrity, upon them, we have been respectful for their blessed innocence which we are told, and have been led, with a cynical apathy, or with the fastidious shrinking of a mistaken refinement, that their fate did not concern us.

"Upon the use of every individual member of the portion of society which the slave trade has made legal, in Coleridge, and as long as society's laws whether sanctioned by act of Parliament or no, ordain a yearly holocaust of thousands of its weakest members, so long will their blood be upon the head of every responsible member of society.

"Yet there is a change coming. There is on the horizon a cloud scarcely bigger than man's thought which is destined to grow and spread till it overawes the earth. It is the awakening of women to their responsibilities toward their young sisters. The truths that are dawn ing with irresistible but gradual force upon the reason ing minds of men have at length begun to affect the grip of the hardened spirits of women, and caused the sexes of age-long custom, the 'stiffening crust' of tradition to fall from their eyes. They are going forth to raise the newly-fallen to melt the ice which will love to wipe out the stains of selfish horror in a future hope and calm; they are moreover working socially for justice, for honesty, for a loftier purity, for the practical extension of the kingdom of Christ."
