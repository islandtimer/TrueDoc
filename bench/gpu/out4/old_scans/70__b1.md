"This is the class that are damned into the world, not by the want of knowledge, facility of science, and reformers to see to it that this class of parents do not drain the life of the parent tree."

Sure are the penalties and responsibilities that result from the sins of ignorance, sensuality and irresponsibility in many families. This may be considered an extreme case. But do we not see such results in a degree all along the line? How often we hear of the ministers of the Gospel and devout Christians, having wild, vicious sons and unreliable daughters, and how often we hear of the "Why should such God-fearing families be so afflicted?"

A great stride forward will have been taken when the religious world will have shewn a decided abatement of the popular error that God creates every child that is born, calls him forth into life by the fiat of His will. Here human responsibility is ignored by the masses. Divine responsibility is ignored by the masses for our sins and shortcomings. But this immunity does not extend to the teaching and improving of domestic animals. The farmer who wishes to teach his steeds to a certainty that if he wishes fine cattle and spirited horses he must be wise in selection, careful to supply them with vigorous food, and give them enough exercise to insure the health and vigor of the female.

The point is aptly illustrated by a correspondent in a recent number of the Women's Journal. The writer, a comrade in arms, says:

"In combination with their own peculiarities; before they reach maturity the children of officers are so helplessly dependent upon their ancestors not only for the breath of life, but for qualities that will make their characters, and even their opinions, conform to the views and society; that will endow them regularly, not impoverish them with disorder and disease; crown them with triumphs, not defeat and failure."

When this great day shall have dawned upon the children of men, the work of the church—Christ's church on earth—will not be the expiation of sin, but the redemption of souls by sprinkling or emersion, while they despise and neglect their bodies, but shall be the healing of the great leprosy of society, the redemption of souls, the expulsion of criminal classes, the care and education of youth by every elevating and salutary influence. Teachers and teachers' assistants by the power of their example, kindness and cultivating virtue and purity in their seals, and so manifest it in their lives that from them will emanate the light of truth, and the power of the Holy Spirit will bear heavy unto them, to heal and bless. They must recognize physical law to be as divine and immutable as spiritual law, and impose upon themselves the necessity that they must cultivate virtue except when manifested through a healthy body. Therefore the attainment and maintenance of physical health be supreme in our view. And we must be able to hand down to our principles of beneficence and purity if we expect to do good to others.
