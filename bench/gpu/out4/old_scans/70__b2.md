This subject is aptly illustrated by a correspondent in a recent number of the Women's Journal. The writer, a wife and mother, is over-burdened by housekeeping and the care of her little children; her husband stupidly oblivious of the fact. She says: "Once we were riding; at the foot of a hill he told me to get out and walk up as he would not ride in July. My baby would be born in August."

He remembered to be kind to his horse and care for his old in embryo. He was quite unconscious of the fact but he had thought for his own child. I fear this is not an unusual case, for, alas, it is the experience of many wives. Another one writes: "I have no money only what I earn from the sly. Money is so small and small is in her husband's pocket she would take it, if she thought he would not miss it. His children will inevitably be sly and thriftless. But he feels no responsibility in the matter. They are God's children not his."

Faithless husbands have proclaimed the doctrine of physical fitness, and have written about the consequences of "under- and over-population." Medical teachers and physicians have recommended marriage or cohabitation as a specific for sexual diseases. All manner of protections have been invented to conceal, with futility (which often means suicide), have been devised. Some have even professed consecration of sexuality, which proves to be another name for senseless indulgence without profit, and to their aid are falshood and hypocrisy propul- sive, that cry "peace, peace, when there is no peace," the magnanimity of sympathy. Like the shedding of tears, wealth or honor may not be impaired by years of abstinence, but like weeping, intoxication depresses nervous energy.

This doctrine of convenience is the stone which all social institutions have rejected or neglected. I now present it as the chief corner-stone of the Institute of Heredity. If accepted, it will set the institute on a firm enduring course.
