# Random entry: eight words against the run-48 failures

Technique: de Bono's random entry. Eight words were handed to me (choir, braille, ledger, telescope, orchestra, compost, ferry timetable, customs officer). For each I listed properties and associations, then forced a connection to a failure group in the evidence pack, harvesting without judging. Judgement comes afterwards, as one objection per idea. Everything below is read-only: no conversions, no OCR, no page checks were run. Where I quote a number it is tagged [verified] (read from a file or computed here from the run-48 outputs and `failed_tests.jsonl`), [derived] (a census I computed with a stated method), or [assumed] (a judgement, mostly hours).

## Part 1: the harvest (word, properties, forced connections)

**Choir.** Many voices, one text; a wrong note from one singer is covered by the rest; parts (soprano, alto, tenor, bass) sing the same words in different registers; a choirmaster settles disputes; unison versus harmony; the hymn sheet everyone reads from. Forced links: the hidden OCR layer is one singer who is mostly right and occasionally flat ("trce-lined", "dclle"); we own two more singers already, the English recogniser and the Chinese-plus-English recogniser that ship with RapidOCR; a dictionary is the choirmaster. Vote per word, not per page. Unison: the same word sung by every line of a dictionary page (consistency). Descant: a small raised line above the melody (footnote markers, already handled).

**Braille.** A fixed grid of six-dot cells; read by touch, not sight; every cell the same size; Nemeth code writes mathematics as a linear stream with explicit "superscript" indicators because touch has no second dimension; contracted braille abbreviates. Forced links: the census printout in Courier is a braille page, every character on a fixed lattice, with `|` and `:` embossed where the rules would be; a formula read by classical OCR has no trustworthy vertical dimension either, so write it the way Nemeth does, linearly, rather than rebuilding fractions and scripts from boxes that were never measured.

**Ledger.** Double entry: every figure appears twice and the books must balance; columns of numbers under headings; a total row; ruled paper; an audit trail. Forced links: a total row is a free check on whether the numbers above it were filed in the right column (relation errors: "a label column offset from its numbers"); a scanned table of figures whose rows add up to its totals has proved itself, whatever the OCR engine's confidence says (the 21 table pages whose OCR read was rejected or wrong).

**Telescope.** Narrow field, high magnification; a finder scope points the main scope; you see old light; resolution is limited by aperture; "telescoping" means nesting. Forced links: our OCR looks at the whole page at 2,000 pixels while most scans are stored far sharper [verified below]; tiny type wants a narrow field at full magnification (tiles), and a doubtful word wants a crop at native resolution (the choir's second and third singers read that crop); old light: the old maths scans were typeset by hand and there are no font metrics to rebuild from.

**Orchestra.** Parts and a full score; bar lines align every part in time; a phrase is held across the bar line; sections enter and fall silent; tuning to the oboe's A. Forced links: a paragraph is a part and must not stop mid-phrase because a caption or footnote "enters"; the score is the reading order and the bar lines are the block boundaries; the 52 sentences broken at a block boundary are parts cut off at the bar line.

**Compost.** Layers turned over; heat; what was refuse becomes soil; sieving keeps the fine material and returns the coarse to the heap; browns and greens in ratio. Forced links: a rejected OCR read is thrown on the heap whole, and the heap includes the printed letterhead, date line and typed captions of a handwritten letter, which are real text; sieve the rejected read line by line. The confidence-and-word-likeness ratio is the browns-to-greens ratio, already tuned at page level, never at line level.

**Ferry timetable.** A grid of departures; rows repeat to a rhythm (time, vessel, note; time, vessel, note); the boat runs both ways, so the same table is printed in the other direction; footnote symbols tie cells to notes; validity dates. Forced links: multi-line cells reveal themselves as a periodic rhythm of baseline gaps (small, small, large); a table printed sideways is the return crossing, read in the turned frame; symbols in cells (the attendance list's ticks) are timetable footnote marks.

**Customs officer.** Checks the passport's format rather than recognising the traveller; classifies goods by a tariff schedule; red and green channels; stamps; asks "anything to declare?"; refuses entry to what is not what it says it is. Forced links: a running head on a single page cannot be confirmed by seeing it on another page, but its passport (the format of a journal line, a form identity line, a domain-and-number stamp) can be read on the spot; a line that appears twice on one page (head and foot) has, in effect, been seen twice; the maths rebuild's output on an OCR page is goods mis-declared as font evidence. A branch that died at the border: the PDF's own metadata as a passport. Every one of the 24 failing header/footer files was re-saved by pypdf and carries no title, subject or author [verified], so nothing can be read from it.

## Part 2: the ideas

### 1. Choir: three voices on every doubtful word

- **Word and movement.** Choir, then "one flat singer is covered by the rest", then "we already own three singers and a choirmaster", then "vote per word where the layer is doubtful".
- **Concept.** Do not re-read a hidden-layer page (tried, page-level swap, set aside). Re-read only the words the layer is doubtful about, with two independent recognisers, and change a word only when two voices agree on the same alternative.
- **Mechanism.** On a page with a hidden OCR layer (and on our own OCR pages), mark a token as suspicious when it is not a dictionary word, or has punctuation glued between letters (`wide,often`, `usualormore`, `company(with`), or mixes digits and letters oddly (`34i`, `I minute`). Render the token's box at the scan's native resolution, run the English recogniser (`models/rapidocr/PP-OCRv3/en_PP-OCRv3_rec_infer.onnx`) and the Chinese-plus-English recogniser (`ch_PP-OCRv3_rec_infer.onnx`, bundled) on the crop; both are installed already [verified]. Voices: the layer's token, recogniser A, recogniser B, and the dictionary (which "sings" any candidate that is a word within one known confusion pair: c/e, l/1, I/1, o/0, rn/m, cl/d, t/f). Replace the token when at least two voices other than the layer agree, and keep the layer's token otherwise. Crops also restore lost spaces, because the English model keeps them. The change is recorded in the front matter as a corrected token with the original.
- **Failure group and size.** Hidden-layer errors and classical-OCR spelling: multi-column near misses (of the 466 snippet instances in the 233 failing order checks, 193 differ from the reference in letters, 17 in spaces or punctuation, 99 are far or missing, 157 are already identical [derived]); tiny text (56 of 90 differ in letters, 8 in spaces or punctuation [derived]); old scans we did read (71 present, 56 order checks [derived]). About two in three of the wrong tokens in the near misses are of the suspicious kind (477 of 707 by the 10,000-word list in `truedoc/data/en_common_words.txt`; a bigger list would flag fewer correct rare words) [derived]. Realistic: 40 to 80 checks across three sections.
- **Cost.** 6 to 10 hours [assumed]: crop rendering exists (`ocr_region`), the second recogniser needs a second engine instance, the vote and the audit note are new.
- **One-hour falsification.** Take the 32 tiny-text near-miss snippets and 30 multi-column ones on hidden-layer pages from `failed_tests.jsonl`; for each wrong token (diff our `truedoc47` output against the reference), crop its box at native resolution and run both recognisers; count corrections that match the reference against corruptions of tokens that were right. Fewer than 60 per cent net correct, or any corruption rate above one in ten, kills it.
- **Strongest objection.** The hidden layer was made from these same pixels by an engine at least as good as ours; on old print both recognisers may share the c/e and l/1 confusions, so "two voices agree" may just be two copies of the same mistake. Per-word crops also lose the line context a recogniser uses.

### 2. Braille, first movement: read the fixed-width printout as a cell grid

- **Word and movement.** Braille, then "every cell the same size on a fixed lattice", then "the census printout is braille: `|` and `:` are the embossed rules".
- **Concept.** On a page whose characters all sit on one lattice, do not look for whitespace channels; the page is a character matrix, and the separator characters that repeat down a column are rulings.
- **Mechanism.** Test the lattice: with pitch = 0.6 x font size, the share of characters whose x-position falls within 0.15 of a lattice point. On the census page (`tables/ff19f6fe...pg4.pdf`, 8-pt Courier) it is 3,264 of 3,264 characters on a 4.8-pt pitch [verified]. Build the matrix (row = baseline, column = lattice index). Columns where `|` or `:` occurs in 10 or more rows are vertical rules (on that page: lattice columns 0, 42, 120 and 148, in 40 to 56 of 64 rows [verified]); rows that are mostly `-` are horizontal rules. Hand these "ASCII rulings" to the ruled-table builder as if they were drawn lines; cells are the text between rules; stacked heading lines above the first rule are joined into one heading cell (the checker joins stacked headings). The guard matters: the two other Courier pages in the tables section are hidden OCR layers that merely report Courier (their positions are not on a lattice), so the lattice test, not the font name, is the trigger. The whole tables section has exactly three Courier-dominant pages, all failing [verified], so nothing passing is at risk.
- **Failure group and size.** Tables present-but-unstructured: 5 checks on the census page (its failing cells include `16 and 17 Years` with `18 to 24 Years` below, and `0` under the heading `Native Hawaiian & Other Pacific Islander Alone`), and the fixed-width family beyond the benchmark (mainframe reports, bank statements, log printouts) which the owner's users will meet.
- **Cost.** 3 to 5 hours [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** Build the matrix for the census page with PyMuPDF's `rawdict` (twenty lines of Python), cut it at the four rule columns, write the markdown table by hand from the cut, and run its five checks through `olmocr.bench.tests.TableTest`. Three or fewer passing means the reference's heading structure is not what the rules give, and the idea is not worth building.
- **Strongest objection.** One page in the benchmark, five checks; the value is in the field, not the score.

### 3. Braille, second movement (Nemeth code): write OCR-page formulas linearly

- **Word and movement.** Braille, then "touch has no second dimension, so Nemeth writes maths as a stream", then "an OCR line has no trustworthy second dimension either".
- **Concept.** On pages read by OCR, stop rebuilding two-dimensional structure. The character boxes there are approximations, each line divided evenly (`ocr/rapid.py`, `_split_words`), so fraction bars, scripts and limits inferred from them are invented. Write formula-shaped lines as a linear stream inside `$...$`, exactly as read.
- **Mechanism.** The maths stage (`pipeline._apply_math`) takes the layout model's formula regions and calls `display_formula_blocks` with no gate on `page.quality.kind` [verified]. On `ocr-truedoc` pages, run instead: a line is formula-shaped when it has an `=`, `:` or `::`, or an operator between single letters, and no dictionary words other than function names (sin, cos, log); write it verbatim as `$...$`, keeping spaces, mapping `--` to `-` and `'` to a prime; inside a prose line, wrap a formula-shaped run (`y = sin x`) inline. No scripts are ever inferred. The evidence that this is needed: our run-48 output for `old_scans_math/...1_pg113` contains `$$\mathrm{h}(2_{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{c}_{2}\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{x}),(^{\mathrm{h}}4^{2}\mathrm{c}2\mathrm{x}...$$` where the page says `h(2c-x)...` [verified]; that is invented structure, which the project's own first principle forbids, on top of losing the check.
- **Failure group and size.** Formulas on scans. The old-scan-maths section's 458 checks are all formula checks (`old_scans_math.jsonl` holds `math` only [verified]), median reference length 27 characters. Of the 439 failing, 173 are plain ASCII (`x = AG`, `u = xy`, `x' = ax + by + cz`), 72 use only simple symbols (`\phi`, `\lambda`), 194 have fractions, roots, sums or matrices [derived]; 69 have no scripts or braces at all, on 16 pages, none of them empty [derived]; 313 of the 439 sit on the 26 pages we do read [derived]. The checker passes an exact string match at once and otherwise compares KaTeX renderings, in which spacing does not matter [verified from `MathTest.run`], so `$u = xy$` written from OCR passes `u = xy`. Ceiling 69; realistic 15 to 40. Each check here is worth 0.22 points of the section, so 30 checks are about 0.8 points overall.
- **Cost.** 2 to 4 hours [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** Run RapidOCR alone (no pipeline) on the 16 pages carrying the 69 script-free formulas and keep the raw lines; count the formulas present verbatim modulo spaces (only 9 of the 69 survive in today's outputs, but today's outputs have been through the rebuild [derived]). Then wrap those lines in `$...$` and score them with `olmocr.bench.tests.MathTest` from the installed package, which settles whether the render comparison accepts `sin` typed as letters. Fewer than 10 of 69 in the raw lines kills it.
- **Strongest objection.** The English recogniser reads `x²` as `x2` and drops small superscripts, so 104 of the plain-ASCII formulas with scripts stay out of reach, and hand-set 1890s type may read too poorly for even the plain ones.

### 4. Ledger: the books must balance

- **Word and movement.** Ledger, then "double entry: every figure is checked by a second figure", then "a total row is a free audit of column assignment and of OCR".
- **Concept.** Arithmetic consistency is evidence the pipeline never uses. A column that adds up to its total is filed correctly; a scanned number table whose rows add up has proved its reading.
- **Mechanism.** (a) In any rebuilt table with a row whose label is Total, Sum, Gesamt, Suma or similar, sum the numeric cells above it per column; if a column fails but succeeds when the numbers are shifted one column (the label column offset from its numbers) or when two adjacent columns are swapped, apply the shift. (b) In `apply_ocr`, when a page is rejected but is mostly numbers, look for total rows in the read; if two or more columns balance to within rounding, accept the read (a balanced ledger is a far stronger witness than a 0.75 mean confidence, because random OCR errors do not balance).
- **Failure group and size.** Table relation errors and OCR'd number tables: 64 numeric-cell failures on 32 pages, 32 of them on 13 pages whose output contains a Total or Sum word [derived]; the 21 table pages with rejected or wrong OCR. Realistic 5 to 15 checks.
- **Cost.** 4 to 6 hours [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** For the 13 pages with a Total word, parse our markdown tables from `truedoc47/tables/`, sum each numeric column and compare with the Total row under the current assignment and under a one-column shift; count columns that balance only under the shift. Zero means (a) is dead; then check (b) on the rejected number-table pages by grepping their OCR reads for a total row.
- **Strongest objection.** Totals are rounded, percentages do not sum, subtotals nest, and "does not balance either way" will be the common outcome; the rule may fire on two pages.

### 5. Telescope: a narrow field at full magnification for tiny type

- **Word and movement.** Telescope, then "resolution is what you paid for", then "we throw most of it away before the OCR looks".
- **Concept.** The OCR pass renders the whole page at no more than 2,000 pixels on the long side (`_MAX_SIDE = 2000` in `ocr/rapid.py`, about 180 dpi on a letter page) [verified]. The scans are sharper than that: of the failing scanned pages, 119 of 133 in old scans (median native long side 3,304 px, carrying 793 failing checks), 27 of 36 in old-scan maths (median 3,583 px), 17 of 35 in tiny text (43 checks), 25 of 58 in multi-column, 20 of 32 in tables are stored above 2,000 px [derived from the embedded images]. The recogniser normalises each line crop to 48 px tall; a 5-pt dictionary entry at 180 dpi is about 12 px tall and is blown up fourfold, blurred; at a native 600 dpi it is about 42 px, sharp.
- **Mechanism.** For pages whose body type is under about 7 pt (tiny text, dictionaries, newspapers) or whose native image exceeds 2,000 px, tile the page (3 x 3 with 10 per cent overlap) rendered at native resolution, OCR each tile, map boxes back, drop duplicates in the overlaps, and stitch lines cut by a tile edge. Feed the result to the choir (idea 1) as a voice against the hidden layer rather than replacing the page.
- **Failure group and size.** Tiny text where the words are not in the layer at all (26 far-or-missing snippets [derived]; the pack's 54) and small type on old scans. Realistic 10 to 20 checks. Caution: the progress log records that rendering four old scans at 3,300 px moved their phrase scores by under two points either way [verified], so this is aimed only at pages where the type is small enough for resolution to bind, not at old scans in general.
- **Cost.** 3 to 5 hours [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** Pick five tiny-text pages whose native image is above 3,000 px; OCR one quarter-page tile at native resolution and the same region cut from the 2,000-px render; count how many of the page's failing snippets each finds. The pack's page-level figure was 13 of 66; if the tiles do not beat the 2,000-px cut on the same region, stop.
- **Strongest objection.** The hidden layer of a tiny-text page was made from these pixels by a stronger engine and still got the words wrong; magnification may only make the same errors sharper.

### 6. Orchestra: hold the phrase across the bar line

- **Word and movement.** Orchestra, then "a part is not cut off because another section enters", then "a sentence is not cut off because a caption sits between its two halves".
- **Concept.** The 52 mid-sentence block breaks have no single cause (footnotes, captions, forms interleave), but they have a single symptom: a block that ends mid-sentence followed by a block that does not continue it, while a later block does. Enforce adjacency of the two halves after ordering.
- **Mechanism.** After `assign_reading_order`, walk the ordered blocks. Block A ends mid-sentence when its last character is a letter, comma, semicolon or hyphen. The next block X fails to continue it when X starts with a capital, a digit, a bullet, a caption or footnote marker, or is a table or figure. A later block B continues A when it starts with a lowercase letter (or a hyphen-completing fragment), has body size, and sits either in A's column below the interlopers or at the top of the next column. Then move B (and the blocks that follow it in its own column) to directly after A, or, equivalently, move the interlopers to after B. Never move a heading, never move across a page-spanning rule.
- **Failure group and size.** Reading order: my census finds 37 to 47 of the 262 missing multi-column snippets whose thirds are all present but far apart in our output, on 31 to 36 pages (thirds matched with edit distance 4 to 6) [derived], consistent with the pack's 52. Realistic 15 to 30 checks.
- **Cost.** 3 to 5 hours [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** On those 35 pages, apply the rule by hand to the failing pairs using the `truedoc47/multi_column` outputs and count where the two parts become adjacent; then read 20 passing multi-column pages for false moves (a sentence that ends without a full stop before a heading is the trap). More than two false moves in twenty pages kills the plain version and calls for a stricter continuation test.
- **Strongest objection.** A continuation that starts with a proper noun or a number ("... by / Smith (1990)") fails the lowercase test, and a list item that starts lowercase passes it wrongly; the rule may fix twenty checks and break ten elsewhere.

### 7. Compost: sieve the rejected heap

- **Word and movement.** Compost, then "refuse contains good soil; sieve it", then "a rejected OCR read contains good lines".
- **Concept.** The gate is page-level: mean line confidence at least 0.75 and half the tokens word-like, else the whole read is discarded and the file is empty [verified in `apply_ocr`]. A handwritten letter's printed letterhead, date line and typed caption are real text and read confidently; they go to the heap with the handwriting.
- **Mechanism.** When the page gate rejects, keep the lines that individually pass a stricter sieve: confidence at least 0.90, language-like share at least 0.7, at least three tokens, not a lone number. Write them in position, with a front-matter warning that the page was only partly readable and the count of lines kept versus discarded, so the reader knows the page is mostly missing. This needs the owner's agreement, since D008 was written for whole pages.
- **Failure group and size.** Empty pages: 76 zero-byte outputs (61 old scans, 10 old-scan maths, 3 headers/footers, 1 multi-column, 1 tables) [verified]. On the empty old-scan pages sit 61 baseline, 180 present and 110 order checks; on the empty old-scan-maths pages 10 baseline and 126 formula checks [derived]. A baseline check passes with any alphanumerics, so every page with one kept line gains one; realistic 20 to 50 baseline and 5 to 20 present checks.
- **Cost.** 2 to 3 hours [assumed], plus the owner's decision.
- **One-hour falsification.** Re-run the OCR census on the 71 empty old-scan pages keeping per-line scores (the 7 September census already did the page-level part); list the lines that pass the sieve; check 40 of them against the page images. Fewer than 90 per cent correct real text kills it; that count is also the owner's evidence for the decision.
- **Strongest objection.** It changes what "empty means unreadable" promises: a reader gets "Dear Sir" and a letterhead and may not notice the letter itself is missing, however loud the front-matter note; and the baseline gain is real on the scoreboard but hollow in meaning.

### 8. Ferry timetable: rows to a rhythm, and the boat comes back the other way

- **Word and movement.** Ferry timetable, then "departures repeat to a rhythm" and "the return crossing is the same table turned", then two table mechanisms.
- **Concept.** (a) Multi-line cells show as a periodic pattern of baseline gaps. (b) A sideways table is the same table in the turned frame; the finders currently drop every rotated line (`if not l.rotated` in `tables/aligned.py`) [verified].
- **Mechanism.** (a) In a table candidate, take the sequence of gaps between consecutive baselines; test periods 2 and 3 for a repeating small-small-large pattern (autocorrelation of the gap sequence, or simply a threshold that splits gaps into two classes and checks that the large ones fall every p lines); when a period holds for at least four cycles, group the lines into rows of p and join a column's p lines into one cell; where the reference instead splits two-line cells into rows, the difference is that the second line has no text in the first column, so require every line of a group to be label-less except the first. (b) Collect rotated lines sharing a direction; if there are ten or more, map their boxes (x, y) to (y, -x), run `table_from_lines` in the turned frame, and place the table where the group sits, with a front-matter note.
- **Failure group and size.** Tables present-but-unstructured and dropped: a handful each, 3 to 8 checks per mechanism. Six failing table pages carry sideways text [derived], but the two I inspected are pictures whose text layer holds only titles or form fields (`fbeb6edc...`, `4db371...`), which is the vision stage's ground, so (b) may touch only the page the pack names.
- **Cost.** 3 to 4 hours each [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** (a) For the failing table pages, print each page's baseline-gap sequence and look for a period; count the checks on pages where one shows. (b) List failing table pages with ten or more rotated text-layer lines and count their checks. If the two counts together are under six checks, do (a) only if the periodic structure is common in the owner's own documents.
- **Strongest objection.** Two-line cells the reference splits into rows and three-line cells it joins have the same rhythm; the label-less test is a guess about the benchmark's habits, not about documents.

### 9. Customs officer: declared identity, checked at the border

- **Word and movement.** Customs officer, then "the passport's format is enough; the officer need not have seen you before", then "a running head's format can be read on a single page".
- **Concept.** Repetition across pages is unavailable; two substitutes are: the format of the line (a tariff schedule of running-head shapes), and repetition within the page (head and foot carrying the same short line).
- **Mechanism.** A tariff schedule applied to short blocks anywhere in the top 15 per cent or bottom 18 per cent of the page, regardless of the 9 per cent zone: journal citation heads (`J. Appl. Cryst. (1974). 7, 222`, at 8.2 per cent of the page height, whose block bottom lies past the zone [derived]); domain-and-path stamps without `www.` (`dodis.ch/31973`, `health.ucsd.edu/jacobs` at 91.6 per cent, which the contact rule misses because it wants `http` or `www.` [verified]); form identity lines carrying a page number on the same line (`Schedule A (Form 990) 2022 ... Page 7`, whose bottom copy at 82 per cent we wrote as a heading at the end of the file [verified]); legal running heads (`Part 2—Offences relating to food Division 1—Serious offences relating to food`, which survived at 3.8 per cent of the page, so the cause is probably the layout model's override in `fuse.py` rather than the zone); landscape pages where 9 per cent of a short page is too little (the Czech timetable head at about 10 per cent). Plus the within-page repetition rule: a line of at most 60 characters that appears twice on one page at different heights is a running mark.
- **Failure group and size.** Running heads on single pages: 27 absent checks, of which the titles (`Programs of Graduate Study...`, `Physics Minor`), the footnotes kept by D017 (six checks), the single digits (`1`, `2`, `98`, checked only in the first or last ten characters) and the email-substring (`euras.lt`) are out of reach; 4 to 6 reachable.
- **Cost.** 2 to 3 hours [assumed].
- **One-hour falsification.** Apply the schedule by hand to the 27 failing strings and to the top and bottom lines of the 240 passing header/footer pages (PyMuPDF `blocks`, first and last three per page); count hits and false hits. Then run `bench/tools/page_check.py` with `--no-layout` on the six candidate pages: if the heads vanish without the layout model, the fix belongs in the fuse step, not in patterns.
- **Strongest objection.** At most 0.1 points overall; the section is at 96.3 and the residue is mostly things the benchmark wants that a reader does not.

## Part 3: the single best bet

**Idea 3, write OCR-page formulas linearly (the Nemeth movement of braille).** Reasons: it is the cheapest to build (2 to 4 hours) and the cheapest to kill (the raw-OCR count takes half an hour with the installed checker); it acts on the lowest-scoring section, where each check is worth four times a multi-column check (30 checks there are about 0.8 points overall); and it removes something the project's own principle forbids, invented scripts and fraction bars on pages where no glyph box was ever measured, so it is right even if it scores nothing. The fact that would flip it: if the raw RapidOCR lines on the 16 pages hold fewer than 10 of the 69 script-free formulas verbatim, the engine cannot read hand-set maths well enough and the idea dies within the first hour.

The bigger upside, at higher cost and risk, is idea 1 (the choir): its pool is two to three hundred wrong snippets across three sections, but its success depends on two recognisers not sharing the same confusions on old print, which only the one-hour test can tell. Ideas 1 and 5 compose: the telescope supplies the crop, the choir decides.

## Part 4: claims in the evidence pack I doubt

1. **"Our own OCR read of those pages is not better than the hidden layer (tried: 13 of 66 strings found against the layer's 9)."** The numbers say the opposite for the strings measured: our read found 13, the layer 9. The conclusion can only hold for a page-level swap that loses elsewhere what it gains here; a per-token merge (idea 1) was not tested, and the figures quoted are evidence for it, not against it.
2. **"Old-scan maths: text and formulas must be present."** The section's 458 checks are all formula checks; there is no text-presence check in `old_scans_math.jsonl` [verified]. Reading the prose of those pages gains nothing there except baseline; only formulas written as LaTeX count. And on the 26 pages we do read, the outputs are not "nothing invented": the maths rebuild turns evenly-divided OCR boxes into scripts and fraction stacks (the `1_pg113` example above), which is a fidelity fault independent of the score.
3. Minor: the pack's "71 empty pages" are the two old-scan sections only; there are 76 zero-byte outputs in all (three more in headers/footers, one each in multi-column and tables) [verified], and each carries a baseline check.

For the record, one pack figure I first doubted and then confirmed: the "about 52 sentences broken at a block boundary". A strict census of mine found 4; a looser one (thirds of the snippet matched with edit distance 4 to 6, found far apart) finds 37 to 47 on 31 to 36 pages, consistent with 52.
